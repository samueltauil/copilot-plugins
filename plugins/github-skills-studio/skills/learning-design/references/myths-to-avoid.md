# Myths to avoid

Claims that sound scientific but are not supported. Do not use them to justify design decisions, and correct them
politely when a requester asks for them.

| Myth | What the evidence says | What to do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Match instruction to visual, auditory, or kinesthetic "learning styles" | The meshing hypothesis lacked the required evidence (Pashler et al., 2008) | Choose the representation that fits the content (a diagram for structure, steps for procedure) and offer accessible alternatives for real needs |
| "We remember 10% of what we read, 90% of what we teach" (learning pyramid) | The percentages have no credible basis and are wrongly attributed to Dale's cone (Lalley & Miller, 2007) | Justify hands-on practice with retrieval, generation, and transfer evidence |
| Attention collapses after ten minutes | Not supported as a universal limit (Bradbury, 2016) | Size steps by task coherence and tested completion time; offer natural pause points |
| Any difficulty is "desirable" | Only difficulties that engage useful processing help (Bjork & Bjork, 2011) | Remove accidental friction; add only deliberate retrieval or variation |
| A passing check means the learner mastered the skill | A check observes an artifact (Messick, 1995) | State what the check observed; assess transfer with a less-supported task |
| Progress bars and celebrations improve learning | Evidence is from consumer and affective settings (exploratory) | Use them for honest orientation and closure, not as a learning claim |
| Adults learn by fundamentally different mechanisms than children | Andragogy is a useful design lens, but its mechanism claims are contested (Moll, 2024) | Design for relevance and autonomy for every learner |
| Growth-mindset phrasing transforms outcomes | Effects are small and context-dependent (Sisk et al., 2018; Yeager et al., 2019) | Give process-focused, specific feedback; skip slogans |
| Learners take exactly four times the expert's time | A useful practitioner heuristic from GitHub Skills, not a law | Measure with testers and the walkthrough test |
