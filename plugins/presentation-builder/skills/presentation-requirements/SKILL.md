# Requirements-driven presentation design

Use this skill when a presentation includes a `requirements/` folder or other evaluation criteria that the deck must satisfy.

## Treat requirements as the source of truth

The requirement files define how the presentation will be evaluated. Before drafting or editing slides:

- read all available criteria
- map each criterion to one or more slides
- make sure no criterion is left uncovered
- reflect the criteria in both on-slide content and speaker notes

## Suggested criteria-to-slide mapping

When requirements match the structure below, use this mapping as a working checklist:

| Criterion | Primary Slides | How to satisfy it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem Clarity | Slides 2, 3 | Show concrete pain points, relatable scenarios, and credible statistics |
| Value Articulation | Slides 4, 5, 6 | Connect each feature to a business outcome |
| Microsoft Differentiation | Slides 7, 8, 10 | Include direct comparison, enterprise strengths, and ecosystem breadth |
| Storytelling & Flow | All slides | Maintain a narrative arc and smooth transitions in notes |
| Proof / Credibility | Slides 6, 9 | Use named customers, specific metrics, or verified analyst proof points |
| Pitch Readiness | All slides, especially notes | Keep a professional tone, objection handling, and a clear CTA |

## Enforce criterion coverage in notes

Every slide's speaker notes should explicitly reinforce at least one criterion. Use notes to explain:

- why the slide exists in the story
- which requirement it supports
- how to transition into the next idea
- how to answer likely stakeholder objections

## Review before finishing

Before considering the deck complete:

- confirm each criterion is covered across the deck
- remove slides that do not contribute to a requirement or story beat
- tighten notes where the evaluation criteria are weak or implicit
- check that the opening, proof, differentiation, and CTA are all clearly represented
